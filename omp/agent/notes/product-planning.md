# Product planning and parallel delivery

Read for a new complete product or a substantial multi-module product change, before implementation dispatch. Reuse the existing product checkpoint and project documentation. Sol owns planning and judgment and writes or maintains planning, task and decision files, including their project-documentation paths; assigned authors own implementation documentation and artifacts. This is the default planning method, not a new approval ceremony or controller.

## Scope and bounded research

Preserve the user's original requirement labels and complete outcomes. State delivery depth, data ownership, local versus shared multi-user/device behavior, destinations and assumptions. Several named owners in browser localStorage do not provide team collaboration. Resolve a consequential ambiguity using existing context or one focused question; do not silently reduce scope.

For a one-sentence new complete-product brief, assign PM bounded exploratory product research and the complete definition before freezing product direction. PM follows the A route: Dean Peters discovery → JTBD → voice-of-customer → opportunity-solution-tree → user-story. It chooses questions about current workarounds, friction, public user feedback, alternatives and useful unmet needs, searches through shared `web_research_v1`, and reads relevant original pages itself. Reuse sufficient applicable evidence, honor no-network instructions or record why research has no product decision value; routine maintenance does not trigger this default. Stop at a reasoned choice or the existing budget, retaining provider failures and consequential gaps. PM records synthesis in its own `omp_research_evidence` branch and hands off URLs, limits, product implications and actual session/receipt IDs in the existing artifact. Sol inspects the native child evidence when accepting conclusions; never submit PM IDs as root receipts or equate retrieval with user validation. No new research team, provider, budget or approval stage.

The PM assignment supplies the exact user request, evidenced hard constraints, Sol's explicitly provisional assumptions and available research. Do not freeze unsupported product surfaces, collaboration boundaries, complete feature lists or exclusions before discovery merely to fit a convenient implementation. PM may challenge provisional assumptions and recommend materially different directions where useful, while preserving user constraints and authorization. Use one bounded pm-worker full product definition by default: users/problem, scenarios, evidence versus assumptions, positioning and innovation recommendations with value/tradeoffs, journeys, roles/data/rules/invariants, state/error/cancel/recovery, UX direction, complete scope/priorities, acceptance and consequential unknowns. PM researches directly and may follow a finding with a targeted question within the existing assignment. It returns precise `WEB_RESEARCH_REQUIRED` gaps only for unavailable access or unresolved material evidence, never starts children. Sol settles remaining choices within the existing assignment flow, not a repeated workshop or an open-ended agent loop. Matt remains deferred for this PM flow; do not add a research fan-out, controller or user-approval stage.

PM is the sole writer of `docs/product/PRD.md` and `docs/product/research.md`; Sol is the sole writer of `docs/product/decisions.md`. The PRD is the product authority, has a version and stable requirement IDs, and is marked complete and reviewable only after it contains the complete scope, source classifications, innovative tradeoffs, all journeys and observable acceptance. Research records sources, limitations, decision links, PM session/receipt identity and unresolved questions. Decisions records Sol's accept/narrow/reject/defer adjudications and the allowed revision scope before the final review snapshot; it links findings, PRD revisions and the current PRD version, but does not receive routine post-check completion marks. Native task/checkpoint or external evidence retains the actual review receipt. An engineering contract references stable PRD IDs and version; it does not copy product rules into a second product authority. Sol resolves cross-file conflicts.

Before accepting the definition, distinguish user evidence, Sol decisions, PM inferences and external evidence; an internal assignment is not user confirmation. Select useful innovations by user value, tradeoff and observable effect, including justified needs beyond the user's wording; do not impose a feature quota or label all routine robustness as innovation. Record acceptance/rejection and reason in the maintained contract, mapping accepted ideas to journeys and acceptance. Check the consequences of each introduced capability: first use, waiting or nonresponse, failed/cancelled work and recovery, where applicable. State what happens to existing data and other participants when a user cannot proceed. Product promises must match actual mechanisms and explicit limits; this check does not mandate new identity services, moderation, arbitration, automatic timeouts or other infrastructure. For a complete product, two independently dispatched native-only product reviewers read the same frozen, complete PRD/research version: `product-reviewer` applies BMAD for value, completeness, scope and handoff; `product-rules-reviewer` applies pm-critic for business states, data changes, exceptions and recovery. Initial reviews are read-only, do not see each other's findings, and cite the same source version, verbatim evidence, consequence and minimum correction; zero findings are valid. Sol verifies citations, deduplicates and adjudicates every finding in `decisions.md`. The original product artifact owner makes accepted revisions; if its native session has ended, a new bounded `pm-worker` revision task cites the original task/artifact and decision record, may write only the two PM-owned files and does not restart research. It identifies each revision task/attempt so a later revision never appears to be the original first delivery. Each reviewer whose area changed independently rechecks the current version. This limited two-review loop ends when accepted blocking issues are resolved, required rechecks are present and material decisions close; it does not create an extra PM team, user approval or unbounded review cycle. Purple PLAN then independently checks the frozen engineering semantic plan and remains required; product review never replaces it.

Record the authorized execution environment in the plan. Docker requires the user's express request; its absence does not authorize a container engine. Under existing product authorization, Sol freezes the minimal verified native environment and does not automatically replace it with an unverified alternative because Docker is unavailable. For a Windows background service, assign one runtime owner, retain data and logs in its `D:/Labs/omp` run directory, launch hidden, verify descendants have no visible consoles and verify lifecycle, then use scoped graceful cleanup only for identified owned processes when authorized run consumers finish or on an explicit user stop. Cancellation never authorizes automatic resume.

## Plan before code

Sol maintains a compact task table in the existing plan/checkpoint and writes or updates its planning, task and decision entries at the relevant project-documentation paths. An assigned author persists useful approved implementation documentation. Each row names: task ID; original requirement labels AND their text; observable deliverable; author role; allowed paths; shared resources/invariants and their single owner; interface inputs/outputs; dependency maturity; planned branch/worktree; focused checks; integration owner; and planned versus actual status, model/effort, worktree, commit and receipt references. Record request, plan-ready, first-effective-author, foundation-ready, dispatch, blocked, handoff and final timestamps in the existing task table/evidence. Aim for first effective author dispatch 15–20 minutes after request; this is nonbinding, and exceeding it diagnoses the concrete blockage without waiving a gate. Do not create a separate plan-transcription assignment. Do not invent requirement numbering in child assignments. Before implementation dispatch, freeze scope, architecture, interfaces, owners, invariants and the parallelism decision in the maintained plan/checkpoint; the dispatch `Decisions` field cites its path and version and never replaces it. Discovery dispatch instead cites the brief, hard constraints and provisional assumptions; it does not require a completed product or architecture contract. Sol maps every accepted innovation to a concrete implementation package and observable acceptance before implementation dispatch. An assigned author that persists implementation documentation cites that source and persists only its approved decisions; new consequential facts return to Sol as `SOL_DECISION_REQUIRED` with evidence and options.

For a multi-module complete product or multi-agent work, use the installed skill://archify to produce module/data-flow and task dependency/concurrency views when the existing project documentation does not already express them. Simple single-owner work may use one compact view or no new diagram when the existing representation remains sufficient. The assigned implementation author may retain necessary Archify JSON and rendered HTML in existing project documentation; a diagram owner is an existing writer and counts within the four-author limit, not a separate role or ceremony. Use actual task IDs, roles, interfaces, owned paths and branch/worktree facts. Text remains authoritative. Sol supplies the frozen semantic plan and inspects the result; Sol does not author implementation artifacts. Purple PLAN independently checks that frozen scope, interfaces, invariants, task table and graph before dependent implementation without waiting for rendering. A real semantic conflict blocks only its affected dependencies; cosmetic rendering or layout work proceeds in parallel and never blocks a verified foundation handoff. Create required views alongside approved implementation and validate them against actual execution before final freeze; final diagrams must be accurate and readable. Update actual execution state from settled receipts, and reconcile final actuals once before final reviews; planned work is not running work, and merged work is not product acceptance. A status change does not require a full screenshot gate.

Explicitly evaluate parallel opportunities. Prefer independent single-author outcomes over one whole-application UI assignment. If serial execution is selected, state the concrete shared invariant, overlapping file or unresolved interface that prevents useful parallel work. An empty repository justifies only initial foundation serialization, not serial execution of the entire product or backend. Immediately after shared-foundation verification, dispatch every ready independent package and record actual remaining path/interface conflicts; reassess again after the UI shell. Stable contracts and explicit fixtures permit provisional parallel independent UI journeys until real integration; shared globals, types and manifests have one owner. Prefer the existing worktree runner for independently accepted packages, but do not force artificial parallelism. The native per-session ceiling is configured separately; runner active authors remain bounded by task-plan-automation.md and controller leases remain independent.

## Foundation, branches, integration

First inspect and reuse the applicable foundation. Assign and verify only the runnable application shell, shared routing/navigation, theme/tokens, minimally reused components, types, operation contracts, persistence primitives, or dependency manifest/lockfile that later work actually needs. It establishes a stable base; it does not absorb whole business journeys, module-specific forms, validation or business rules. Sol settles cross-module semantics before dispatch, including ownership, lifecycle and consistency rules that change another module's outcome. Small single-author work can combine initial setup with its first capability. Give shared files one owner. Validate a risky interface through a narrow end-to-end path before broad dispatch. An agreed interface can support independent UI authoring against explicit fixtures, but those fixtures remain provisional until real integration; real upstream code requires the declared verified commit or integrated result before dependent execution.

The foundation handoff is concrete: record its verified commit and actual root/cwd, install/start and focused-check commands, the frozen locked-install command where dependencies exist (for example `bun install --frozen-lockfile`), shared types/props/callbacks or service interfaces, route/navigation entry points, reusable component paths, and the one owner for routing, App shell, manifests, lockfiles, global styles and the storage core. Expose persistence through frozen module-facing operations so business authors do not all edit one central store. Name the integration owner, integration target, required input commits, exact combined-journey checks and done condition. For runner candidates, record the plan's `candidateSetupCommand` argv and the independent setup receipt; it runs in the merged candidate's actual cwd immediately before the frozen candidate check, with no retry. The handoff must identify every remaining independent journey, its exact paths, inputs/interfaces, acceptance, owner and conflicts. “Reassess later” is not a handoff or a serial-execution reason.

For the early real path, assign a source/test author and a separate runtime owner, then exercise login, create, edit, reload, and persisted-state confirmation where those requirements apply. Keep regression source with the product and per-run data, credentials, logs, traces, and screenshots in a new unique `D:/Labs/omp` directory. Record quoted absolute retained-evidence paths and, before the task table changes to complete, verify that each exists, is nonempty, and belongs to the verified current run. Reviewer artifacts within that run are valid and protected; do not include shared-cleanup artifacts. Record this check. Sol assigns one writing owner for shared manifests, lockfiles, and contracts. After this foundation path, immediately dispatch dependency-ready, non-conflicting packages within the existing four-author limit and record the actual remaining path/interface conflicts; do not raise configured ceilings or extend the native task runtime or budget to accommodate oversized work.

For two or more independently accepted writing packages, prefer dedicated branches and real worktrees using `task-plan-automation.md`, `task-graph.md` and `merge-queue.md`. Sol approves the concrete plan under the user's existing implementation authorization and may invoke the documented runner; do not ask for duplicate approval for ordinary local branching. Check workspace ownership first and settle parent writers. Never rely on assignment prose to change a native task's cwd. Shared resources serialize writers even if their paths differ. Unrelated branches may proceed within existing limits.

For a new or unstarted remaining plan, after the foundation is verified, immediately dispatch each ready non-conflicting package: use a regular native task for serial work, and use the existing runner with `--concurrency 4` when at least two dependency-ready packages have disjoint paths, no conflicting ownership or writes to shared resources or invariants, frozen interfaces, a clean target and settled writers. Under the new `integration-v1` execution policy, refill an available author slot with the next ready non-conflicting package; legacy runner waves remain legacy behavior until their execution policy explicitly changes. If a package is blocked, record the concrete blocked condition without delaying ready independent work, then reevaluate it after the next milestone. The actual task table records only worktree, branch and author observations from receipts; planned work is not created or running, and labels must not be invented. Preserve the existing caps, gates and active-run boundaries.

For ready UI journeys, the existing runner plan names `frontend-worker` on each independent package and Sol invokes that existing runner immediately after the foundation handoff. This creates the real worktrees and dispatches the actual authors; a task table or a later reassessment alone is not dispatch. Business packages should complete their assigned journey in module-owned paths and through the frozen interfaces without routine edits to routing, App shell, globals, manifests, lockfiles or storage core. If fewer than two journeys are ready, record the exact shared path, invariant or immature input that prevents the second package, without blocking any ready work.

Split implementation into independently verifiable business capabilities or UI features, then assign tasks by stable interfaces and ownership. Do not assign an entire frontend or backend to one author when it contains independent ready packages; do not fragment work into individual files or trivial edits merely to increase task count. Several frontend-worker or task authors may run at once under the same role routing. Each package needs frozen inputs/interfaces, exact owned paths, acceptance checks, dependencies, and a bounded handoff. Shared UI foundations, contracts, migrations, manifests and lockfiles have one named writing owner; dependent packages wait for the required maturity. Declare shared database, port and service resources or isolate them per task. Use the available ready work; do not invent work or bypass a dependency to fill slots. Sol coordinates at most four active writing assignments across routes; this is a workflow ceiling, not a machine-wide runtime semaphore. Authors do not recursively dispatch additional writers. Final reviews and repair packages use the same ownership limits, and overlapping repairs wait for settled writers.

The current runner accepts task, sonic, frontend-worker, fast-worker and repair-worker; under `integration-v1`, it keeps up to four active authors and refills each available slot with the next dependency-ready, non-conflicting package. Historical legacy attempts retain their recorded wave behavior. Each task carries the six assignment fields in its prompt, including exact requirement text, interface and check/handoff budget. Do not change runner concurrency or extend time limits to fit oversized tasks. Under the current 30-minute cap, plan checking/repair and a handoff near minute 25; authors report remaining scope before timeout. After interruption, inspect and reconcile the previous author's state before assigning only the unfinished portion.

Declare the integration owner and final journey checks before parallel work starts. The runner coordinates Git commits and serial candidate merges; product integration/glue has its assigned author, while a confirmed post-implementation conflict or defect transfers to repair-worker after the prior author settles. A failed author/check/merge stops dependent dispatch and preserves branches/evidence. No force merge, automatic retry, cleanup, or takeover. A semantic or Git conflict requires inspected evidence and a revised bounded repair assignment under the existing recovery rules.

## Completion

Separate author checks, delivered branch, verified merge and parent product acceptance. The runner's technical/security receipts do not satisfy the parent product checkpoint and do not replace the parent's current-source red security-reviewer, blue verifier, purple-reviewer and applicable visual/experience-reviewer receipts. After integration, assign any required glue and obtain the independent final checks from the actual target source and documented launch. Report unmet requirements and evidence gaps instead of declaring success.

Apply to new plans. Do not reassign active authors, mutate accepted or frozen runs, or discard an existing product's accepted scope. For an existing session, finish/reconcile active assignments first, then replan only the remaining work.

## Planning artifact quality

Retain both required Archify JSON source and rendered interactive HTML artifacts before final reviews and delivery. Use the simplest supported topology and template that remain semantically complete; keep the upstream showcase correctness and visual-check profile. Bound layout iteration to two attempts. After two failed layout attempts, preserve the diagnostic evidence and hand it to Sol or Terra instead of changing renderer internals indefinitely or relaxing the quality profile. Artifact rendering is never a hard blocker for the independent semantic PLAN review.
